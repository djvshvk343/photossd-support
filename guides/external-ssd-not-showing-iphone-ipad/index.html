External SSD Not Showing on iPhone or iPad?

Reviewed 29 October 2026 · PhotoSSD support

First check whether the SSD appears in the Files app. This separates a device or connection problem from a library-opening problem in PhotoSSD.

1. Look in Files before opening PhotoSSD

Connect the SSD, open Files and choose Browse. Look for the drive under Locations. If you are already inside a folder, return to the Locations list. Apple explains the connection steps in its external storage guide for iPhone.

If the drive is not visible here, PhotoSSD cannot resolve the underlying connection for you. Continue with the checks below.

2. Check the connection and power

Use a data-capable cable and an adapter suitable for your device. A plug that fits is not evidence that the entire connection supports the drive. Check the accessory manufacturer’s instructions.

Some storage connections need external power. Apple describes powered USB hubs for USB-C connections and a powered Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter for relevant Lightning devices. The exact setup depends on your hardware.

3. Check the drive on your Mac

Confirm that the SSD mounts and that the intended .photoslibrary is present. If the drive also fails to mount on the Mac, investigate the drive or cable before changing the library.

Filesystem support and Mac Photos library storage requirements are different questions. Do not reformat a drive as a quick fix: reformatting removes its contents. Review the library preparation guide and keep a separate backup before considering storage changes.

4. If Files sees the SSD but PhotoSSD cannot open the library

Select the actual .photoslibrary package rather than an exported image folder. Confirm that the transfer to the SSD completed. If originals were stored only in iCloud, prepare a locally available library on the Mac before trying to browse those originals from the drive.

Reopen PhotoSSD and select the library again after reconnecting the drive. A visible SSD does not confirm support for every library version or media item.

5. Send a useful support report

  • Your iPhone or iPad model and OS version.
  • The PhotoSSD version.
  • SSD model and format, if known.
  • Cable, adapter and power arrangement.
  • Whether the drive appears in Files.
  • The error message and the macOS version used for the library.

Contact developer.inquiry.5724@gmail.com. You do not need to send your photo library, personal photos or sensitive filenames. A short description of where the process stops is a useful starting point.
